**The Journey to 2nd Place at MRCC**

Building this flight computer was far more than an exercise in wiring sensors and writing code—it was an intense, high-stakes journey defined by late nights, rapid troubleshooting, and relentless teamwork. Competing in the **Malaysia Rocket Competition (MRCC)** pushed every single member of our team to their absolute technical and mental limits.

From early breadboard prototypes to the final countdown on the launch pad, every milestone demanded rigorous engineering. We spent countless hours resolving SPI bus conflicts between LoRa and SD storage, fine-tuning FreeRTOS multi-task scheduling, and refining our sensor fusion algorithms to guarantee precise, real-time apogee detection.

Launch day delivered its own trial by fire. Facing heavy 433 MHz RF channel saturation and cross-talk on the crowded flight line, we had to remain disciplined under pressure—debugging live serial telemetry streams, optimizing data structures, and ensuring our ground station maintained lock throughout the flight window. Every branch of our team—avionics, recovery, airframe structures, and ground ops—worked as a single synchronized unit when it mattered most.

All of that grit and technical discipline paid off: **our team proudly secured 2nd Place overall at MRCC.**

Standing on the podium with a successful flight record and complete telemetry logs in hand is a testament to what a dedicated team can achieve when facing real-world engineering obstacles. We are leaving this repository fully open-source so the next generation of student engineers, cadets, and rocketry teams can build upon our work, skip our trial-and-error phases, and fly even higher.

Thank you to everyone who supported our team throughout this project campaign. See you at the next launch pad!
